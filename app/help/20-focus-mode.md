# Focus mode

**▶ Focus** shows one task at a time with a visual timer.

## How it works

Press **▶ Focus** in the header (or on a task, to start there). The overlay shows:

- **RIGHT NOW, JUST THIS:** the task, with where it sits in its task tree above it.
- A **depleting dial** in the style of a Time Timer, with a real clock in the middle.
- A **shrinking colour block**.
- **Elapsed and remaining** time, side by side.
- **Staged cues** as time runs down (wrap up, find a stopping point, time) with different sounds.

Pick which timer you see under *Timer style* in ⚙ Settings.

Buttons: **⏸ Pause**, **↺ Reset timer** (start this block over), **+5 min**, **✓ Done**, and **Next task →**.

**It walks the task tree depth-first**: a task's subtasks come before the task itself, so a session drills down to the smallest first step and works back up.

**Closing the overlay only minimises it.** The timer keeps running. A **▶ pill** in the header shows the countdown; click it to come back. The session survives a page reload. **⏹ End session** ends it for good.

## Why it works this way

One task at a time, because a list of twelve is a list you stare at.

**Time is shown in several ways** because it is hard to feel. The dial and block let you see time passing out of the corner of your eye; elapsed and remaining together answer both "how long have I been at this" and "how long do I have".

**Depth-first** because the smallest step is the one you can actually start.

**Minimise, not close**, because you will remember something mid-task. Ducking out to add it should not cost you your countdown.

Focus picks by urgency, whatever the list's [sort](10-sorting-and-ordering.md) is, unless you have arranged the list by hand.
