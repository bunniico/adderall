# XP and levels

Finishing a task pays XP for every minute of work in it.

## How it works

Each task has an **XP rate**, set by its impact and effort:

| | XP a minute |
|---|---|
| Base | 0.25 |
| Each point of impact | +0.10 |
| Each point of effort | +0.05 |

An unrated task earns 1 XP a minute. The most important, hardest work earns 1.75.

A finished task pays its rate for every minute of its **padded estimate** (the estimate plus the [time tax](12-estimates-and-time-tax.md)). A task nobody estimated counts as 30 minutes. Open a task to see its rate and what it will pay.

**Speed bonus.** If you time a task (with ▶ Start or [Focus mode](20-focus-mode.md)) and finish inside its padded estimate, it pays up to 20% more. The bonus grows as you get closer to your raw estimate and is full once you are inside it. Going faster than that earns nothing extra, and running over costs nothing.

Your level and a progress bar sit in the top-right corner. When XP lands, the bar slides and `+62 XP` floats up under it; passing a level runs the bar out to full before starting the next one.

Levels get steadily further apart: 100 XP to reach level 2, another 200 to reach level 3, and so on.

The side rail shows two averages:

- **Average XP / minute**, across every estimated task that has ever paid out. This is the number to raise: it goes up when you spend your time on work that matters and when tasks go to plan.
- **Average daily XP** over the last 30 days, counting quiet days. The Overview repeats this one as a tile.

The Done list keeps what each task paid. The whole thing, confetti included, is one switch in ⚙ Settings: *Gamification*.

## Why it works this way

**Paid by the minute** so an hour on something important is worth more than an hour of trivia. A flat amount per task would make a five-minute email worth twenty times more per minute than a two-hour report.

**Splitting a task changes nothing.** Ten steps of six minutes pay the same as one hour-long task. Breaking work down should help you start, never inflate the score.

**Urgency does not pay.** It still decides what the list puts first, but paying for it would pay you to let things go overdue.

**Effort pays extra** because starting the task you dread is the hard part. The list puts easy wins first to get you moving, and the pay makes up for the hard ones.

**The speed bonus is small and capped** so it rewards a task going to plan, not rushing. It is paid on the estimate, never on the time you took, so working slowly is not rewarded and being slow is not punished.

**XP already earned stays earned.** A task that has paid once never pays again, however often it is reopened, and deleting it later costs you nothing.

**Containers pay through their steps**, never twice for the same afternoon's work.

**The daily average counts quiet days** so it shows the pace the month really ran at, not what a good afternoon pays.
